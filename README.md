# Luc-Baudoin-Fankoua
I am a Health Data Scientist and Data Manager focused on leveraging data science, machine learning, and real-world health data to improve clinical decision-making and health system performance in low-resource settings.

I currently serve as a Data Manager and Research Lead at Douala General Hospital (Cameroon) under the Data Science Without Borders (DSWB) initiative. My work centers on large-scale health data standardization using the OMOP Common Data Model, as well as building end-to-end data pipelines and reproducible analytics workflows across multiple clinical domains including respiratory diseases, tuberculosis, HIV, and hemodialysis.

My expertise spans clinical epidemiology, machine learning, and health information systems. I develop predictive models for outcomes such as in-hospital mortality, treatment response, and patient eligibility, while also designing dashboards and decision-support tools using platforms like Power BI and Dash. I actively work with systems such as DHIS2 and SQL-based ETL pipelines to enable scalable and interoperable health data solutions.

My research interests lie at the intersection of artificial intelligence, public health, and health systems strengthening. Current projects include early prediction of in-hospital mortality using admission data, machine learning models for hemodialysis eligibility and outcomes, prediction of HIV prevention behaviors (PrEP uptake), and evaluation of Community, Rights, and Gender (CRG) frameworks in tuberculosis programs.

Beyond research, I am deeply involved in capacity building across Africa, mentoring health professionals, epidemiologists, and data scientists through workshops, hackathons, and applied training programs in data science and AI for healthcare.

My goal is to develop scalable, data-driven solutions that transform healthcare delivery and improve patient outcomes across Africa.
